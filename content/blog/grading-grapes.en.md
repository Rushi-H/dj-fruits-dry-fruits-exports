## Sizing, Sweetness, and Handling at Harvest

When selling fresh table grapes to an export packhouse, field preparation determines whether your lot passes export inspection or is downgraded to local market table grade.

### 1. The Core Export Specifications
- **Berry Diameter**: Export consignments (Europe & Gulf) generally require a minimum berry diameter of **16mm to 18mm+** for Thompson Seedless, Sonaka, and Super Sonaka.
- **Brix (Sugar Content)**: Refractometer testing must measure a minimum of **16° to 18° Brix**. Harvesting under-ripe fruit results in sour flavor and immediate consignment rejection at port.
- **Color & Uniformity**: Bunches must exhibit an even translucent green-amber tint with clean natural bloom intact. Avoid touching berries directly; hold clusters only by the main stem.

### 2. Harvest Field Discipline
Harvesting should occur strictly during the cool hours of early morning (6:00 AM to 10:30 AM). High afternoon temperatures cause rapid dehydration and post-harvest berry drop.

1. Use sanitized curved grape shears. Never yank bunches by hand.
2. Clip clusters with 2-3 cm of green stem attached.
3. Place bunches gently in sanitized plastic harvest crates lined with ventilated bubble wrap or foam sheets.
4. Stack crates no more than 4 high to prevent lower box crushing.

### 3. Pre-cooling and Transport
Grapes must reach the packhouse pre-cooling tunnel within 3 to 4 hours of picking to drop core pulp temperature from 28°C down to 4°C, preserving stem hydration and preventing berry shatter.
